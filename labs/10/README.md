# Labs — Phase 10, security & supply chain + CKS

Twenty-six exercises in the order they are meant to run, ending on **an incident that destroys the cluster it ran on**. The phase teaches security as a stack of controls read one at a time — an RBAC verb, a projected token, an admission webhook, an encrypted etcd byte, a signed digest, a kernel syscall — and then, in [the capstone](26-the-cve-incident.md), fires all of them at once against a real CVE in a privileged DaemonSet. Nothing about the exploit is softened and nothing about it is committed: the working payload lives only on the isolated cluster and dies with teardown, and what survives in the repo is a defanged postmortem and a Falco rule that had to beat an unseen variant to earn its place.

Each file states one claim to test or one artifact to produce, links its [topology](../../strands/lab-topologies.md#pair) rather than restating a footprint, and ends with a teardown that deletes what it created and says whether the topology stays or goes. The framing — why each module exists, what to read and the question to answer from it — stays in [`phases/10-security.md`](../../phases/10-security.md). These files hold only what you type and what you should see.

## Order, topology and what arrives when

**Six exercises need no cluster.** [Exercise 9](09-rego-that-decides-off-cluster.md) (`opa eval`) and the four supply-chain exercises [16](16-an-sbom-that-shrinks.md)–[19](19-a-signature-with-no-private-key.md) run entirely on [`hopper`/`forge`](../../strands/lab-topologies.md#build-guest) — the supply-chain module is [zero-cluster-cost by design](../../phases/10-security.md#m10-4), which is what makes this phase affordable next to Falco. `pair` may be up and idle beside them.

**One cluster runs everything else.** [`pair`](../../strands/lab-topologies.md#pair) is [provisioned at exercise 1](01-one-verb-one-resource.md) and held for the whole phase; [the capstone](26-the-cve-incident.md) is what destroys it, and [the CKS drill block](25-cks-drill-block.md) then provisions its own fresh `pair`.

**Three tools take up residence and stay.** Kyverno arrives at [exercise 7](07-kyverno-on-the-chain-you-already-read.md) (~320Mi, one replica of each controller, cleanup and reports controllers off) and is still there for [exercise 20](20-verifyimages-rejects-the-unsigned.md). The `EncryptionConfiguration` and audit policy from [exercises 12](12-a-secret-that-is-no-longer-plaintext-on-disk.md)–[13](13-an-exec-that-writes-a-line-to-the-audit-log.md) are control-plane keepers. Falco arrives at [exercise 22](22-falco-reads-the-syscall.md) — [512Mi/node](../../research/ecosystem-picks.md#falco), so it is **pinned to the worker only** and every workload after it is scheduled there, halving the footprint to one node — and stays through the capstone.

## Exceptions

- **The capstone commits no working exploit.** [Exercise 26](26-the-cve-incident.md) exploits `CVE-2025-59359` on the isolated cluster and commits only a defanged postmortem and a Falco rule; the payload dies with teardown, and [the repo's privacy is explicitly not load-bearing](../../phases/10-security.md#capstone). Egress is dropped for the exploit window and the PoC is self-contained — no second-stage fetch.
- **10.C5 is not the last exercise of its module.** The other four drills close their modules ([10.C1](05-10c1-a-wildcard-and-the-path-it-opens.md), [10.C2](10-10c2-a-policy-that-matches-the-wrong-kind.md), [10.C3](15-10c3-encryption-that-lies.md), [10.C4](21-10c4-a-digest-that-no-longer-matches.md)), but [10.C5](23-10c5-a-string-rule-a-variant-evades.md) sits *before* [exercise 24](24-a-falco-rule-that-names-the-container.md), because the drill exposes the string-rule weakness that exercise 24 fixes — and 24, the capstone's detector, has to come last.
- **Four exercises leave a control-plane component edited mid-file.** [The authorizer chain](04-an-authorizer-chain-a-flag-list-cannot-express.md), [encryption at rest](12-a-secret-that-is-no-longer-plaintext-on-disk.md), [the audit policy](13-an-exec-that-writes-a-line-to-the-audit-log.md) and [10.C3](15-10c3-encryption-that-lies.md) all edit the apiserver static manifest. Each confirms the apiserver came back (`kubectl get --raw /healthz`) before declaring success — a malformed edit takes the control plane down, and "passed" and "not answering" are different states.
- **10.C3 ends in a repair, not a delete.** [Exercise 15](15-10c3-encryption-that-lies.md) leaves the cluster writing plaintext Secrets; its teardown restores the correct provider order and re-verifies one Secret encrypts, because leaving the lying config in place would poison every later Secret.
- **Two numbers are recorded, not quoted.** [The kube-bench check ID](11-kube-bench-a-finding-you-can-fix.md) tracks the CIS version your report actually cites, and [exercise 8](08-seccomp-and-apparmor-a-blocked-syscall.md) records which AppArmor pod-spec field (`appArmorProfile` vs the beta annotation) your Kubernetes version honours. Both are version-dependent; assuming instead of recording defeats the exercise.
- **Nothing observability-shaped is installed.** Evidence is an etcd hexdump, an audit line, a `cosign verify` output, a Falco alert, a `curl` that times out. Falco is the only agent, and it is there to be studied, not to watch.
