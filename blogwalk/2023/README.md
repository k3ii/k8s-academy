# 2023 — the census

78 posts, 2023-01-02 to 2023-12-20. Kubernetes 1.27, 1.28 and 1.29 are in scope, with 1.26's
January posts and its release interview arriving alongside. Only 2016's 90 is bigger among the
years censused so far — see [the method](../README.md).

**Yield: 13 `walk`**, upper-[band](../README.md#the-budget), level with 2022 and 2024 and behind only
2016's 15. 51 `read`, 2 `dated`, 12 `skip`.

**A new floor, and a flat year.** The reject rate is 15%, against 48%, 42%, 38%, 40%, 37%, 41%,
21%, 17% and 19% for the years already censused. `meta` takes 13 rows and 17%, also the lowest
share recorded. 65 of the 78 posts are about how the software behaves. The release clustering
2021 introduced keeps loosening: the three busiest months carry 41% here, against 2021's 62% and
2022's 55%, and no month carries more than twelve posts. This is the most evenly spread year in
the archive, and also the largest one the census has reached since 2016.

**A second feature-gate registry.** Nine years of census have treated the 488-file feature-gates
directory as the instrument that says what a gate did. This year breaks it.
[kubeadm: Use etcd Learner to Join a Control Plane Node Safely](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2023/09/25/kubeadm-use-etcd-learner-mode/)
announces `EtcdLearnerMode`, and that gate has no file among the 488, no row in the removed-gates
page, and no entry in either of the two feature-gate tables. It lives in a markdown table inside
`kubeadm-init.md`, which records it as alpha 1.27, beta 1.29, GA 1.32 and removed 1.33. That
table is the whole of kubeadm's gate namespace: one live gate, `RootlessControlPlane`, alpha
since 1.22 and deprecated in 1.31, plus eight removed ones. Nine gates in a command reference
page, against 488 in a directory — and a full ladder that the census's main instrument cannot
see at all.

**"Beta" in a title is not a promise the gate was on.** 34 of the 487 gate files with stage
tables repeat a stage, and the repeat almost always marks a change of `defaultValue` rather than
a change of stage. Two of this year's posts announce the *first*, off-by-default half of such a
split: `NodeSwap` is beta and `defaultValue: false` for 1.28 and 1.29, then beta and
`defaultValue: true` from 1.30, and `SELinuxMountReadWriteOncePod` is beta and off for 1.27
alone, then beta and on from 1.28. Read only the stage column and both look like ordinary
promotions. The census has no way to say "beta, but you still have to ask for it".

**And there is a fourth rung.** `VolumeAttributesClass`, this year's alpha in
[Kubernetes 1.29: VolumeAttributesClass for Volume Modification](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2023/12/15/kubernetes-1-29-volume-attributes-class/),
reaches stable at 1.34 and then takes a *second* stable stage at 1.36 carrying `locked: true`.
49 gates at the pin carry that field. Locked is not alpha, beta or stable; it is the point after
which the switch cannot be set the other way, and the taxonomy's three stages cannot name it.

**One of the instrument's own files contradicts itself.** `ContextualLogging` — the gate behind
[Contextual logging in Kubernetes 1.29](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2023/12/20/contextual-logging-in-kubernetes-1-29/)
— has an alpha stage from 1.24 with no `toVersion` followed by a beta stage from 1.30. Under the
reading rule the census has used since 2016, a last stage with no `toVersion` means "still current
at the pin", so the file asserts both that the gate is alpha-from-1.24-onward and that it is
beta-from-1.30-onward. Four of the 487 files have a non-terminal stage that never closes; this is
one of them, and it is the only one a censused post depends on. Fourteen releases in flight and
still not stable.

**The tree cites the blog back.** Every year so far, the blog has been the only surviving record
of something the documentation forgot. This year runs the other way once.
[Free Katacoda Kubernetes Tutorials Are Shutting Down](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2023/02/14/kubernetes-katacoda-tutorials-stop-from-2023-03-31/)
left six `*-interactive-gone.html` files under `docs/tutorials/kubernetes-basics/` — one per
interactive tutorial — and each of the six links this post as the reason the button is gone.
Permanent tombstones in the docs tree whose authority is a blog post.

**Two repository migrations complete, and neither is reversible.** `apt.kubernetes.io` and
`yum.kubernetes.io` have zero occurrences in the docs tree at the pin; `pkgs.k8s.io` has 31
across five files. `k8s.gcr.io`, which 2022 spent an exercise on, is likewise at zero. Three
posts here carry the package half of that arc, and two of them —
[pkgs.k8s.io](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2023/08/15/pkgs-k8s-io-introduction/) and
[Kubernetes Legacy Package Repositories Will Be Frozen](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2023/08/31/legacy-package-repository-deprecation/)
— carry the same dated self-annotation, an `ℹ️ Update (March 26, 2024)` added months after
publication.

**`etcd` stops being an empty topic.** Nine censused years had produced three `etcd` rows and no
exercise at all — the longest hole in the vocabulary. 2023 adds two rows and the first `etcd`
exercise, and the two arrive together: SIG etcd is founded in November, and the kubeadm learner
post in September is what the exercise walks. `obs` gets its first exercises since 2020, taking
two. `net` takes six rows and no exercise, because 2021's Gateway API walk already covers the
group-version every one of this year's Gateway posts depends on.

**Topics.** `meta` 13, `storage` 11, `security` 11, `nodes` 10, `tooling` 9, `api` 8, `net` 6,
`sched` 3, `etcd` 2, `history` 2, `obs` 2, `ecosystem` 1. Walks: `nodes` 4, `api` 2, `obs` 2,
`storage` 2, `etcd` 1, `security` 1, `tooling` 1.
