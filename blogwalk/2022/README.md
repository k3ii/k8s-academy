# 2022 — the census

69 posts, 2022-01-07 to 2022-12-30. Kubernetes 1.24, 1.25 and 1.26 are in scope, with 1.23's
release interview arriving in April. Only 2016's 90 and 2018's 70 are
bigger among the years censused so far — see [the method](../README.md).

**Yield: 13 `walk`**, upper-[band](../README.md#the-budget), level with 2024 and behind only 2016's 15. 43 `read`, 1 `dated`, 12 `skip`.

**The reject rate reaches its floor.** 17%, against 48%, 42%, 38%, 40%, 37%, 41%, 21% and 19% for
the years already censused — the lowest in the archive. `meta` takes 14 rows and 20%, also the
lowest share recorded. 55 of the 69 posts are about how the software behaves, and the year is not
short: it is the volume of *technical* posts that grew, not the volume of community writing. The
clustering 2021 introduced holds but loosens — the three busiest months carry 55% against 2021's
62% — because 2022 spreads its release work over four months instead of three. May, August,
September and December carry 49 of the 69 posts between them, and December alone carries 16.

**The dockershim arc closes, and it takes five posts across three years to do it.** The pinned
tree holds exactly five posts with *dockershim* in the title: the 2020 deprecation FAQ, 2021's
[Dockershim removal is coming. Are you ready?](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2021/11/12/are-you-ready-for-dockershim-removal/),
and three in this year — a statement of what Docker and Mirantis will keep supporting, an updated
FAQ, and a history. A sixth post, 08, is the upgrade checklist under a different title. The
removal itself lands in 1.24, and the pin's deprecation guide has **no v1.24 section at all**:
the removal this project spent three years preparing people for left no row in the place where
removals are recorded, because dockershim was a component and the guide only tracks APIs. The
seven API removals that *are* recorded arrive one release later, in 1.25.

**A genre that exists only in 2022.** Two posts in the whole 767-post corpus are titled
"Historical Context" — [Dockershim: The Historical Context](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2022/05/03/dockershim-historical-context/)
and [PodSecurityPolicy: The Historical Context](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2022/08/23/podsecuritypolicy-the-historical-context/) —
and both are here, each published within weeks of the removal it describes. The project spent this
year writing the history of its own deletions while performing them. 2021 gave PodSecurityPolicy
a *deprecation* post about its future; 2022 gives it an obituary.

**Three ladders restart rather than continue, and the tree keeps exactly one receipt.**
[Kubernetes 1.25: PodHasNetwork Condition for Pods](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2022/09/14/pod-has-network-condition/)
announces a gate called `PodHasNetworkCondition` setting a condition called `PodHasNetwork`. At
the pin both names are gone: the gate is `PodReadyToStartContainersCondition`, its stage table
begins at alpha 1.28, and it reached stable at 1.37 — the pin's own release. Its file is the
**only one of 488 feature-gate files that carries a `former_titles:` field**, and what it records
is this post's identifier.
[alpha support for running Pods with user namespaces](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2022/10/03/userns-alpha/)
announces `UserNamespacesStatelessPodsSupport`; the pin has `UserNamespacesSupport`, alpha from
1.28, stable at 1.36, and no record anywhere of the old name.
[Alpha API For Dynamic Resource Allocation](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2022/12/15/dynamic-resource-allocation/)
is written against `resource.k8s.io/v1alpha1` with vendor-defined parameters; DRA was redesigned
around structured parameters, its gate's ladder begins again at alpha 1.30, and it went stable at
1.34 on `resource.k8s.io/v1`. Three alphas from one year that were replaced rather than promoted.
The census has been reading ladders as monotonic since 2019; this year says they are not.

**The stationary rungs, and one that is stationary at patch level.**
[Maximum Unavailable Replicas for StatefulSet](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2022/05/27/maxunavailable-for-statefulset/)
announces an alpha in 1.24 that is **alpha from 1.24 through 1.34** — eleven releases — and beta
only from 1.35, still not stable at 1.37. Its beta entry is split into two ranges at a patch
boundary, 1.35.0–1.35.3 and 1.35.4 onward; exactly two gates of 488 carry a patch-level stage
version at all, and this is one of them. `ContainerCheckpoint`, announced at 56, has been beta
since 1.30 and is beta still: eight releases on the middle rung. Against them, `GRPCContainerProbe`
went beta to stable to retired inside four releases of the post announcing it.

**Four subjects left no trace, and one erased its predecessor.** `kueue` has zero occurrences at
the pin, `objectstorage.k8s.io` has zero, `kluctl` has zero — a SIG project, a proposed API group
and a tool, all alive elsewhere and none of them in the documentation. The fourth is the reverse:
`k8s.gcr.io`, the registry every manifest in this archive older than November 2022 points at, also
has **zero occurrences**, while `registry.k8s.io` has 99 across 47 files. The rename did not just
land; it removed the old name from the tree entirely, which is why 53 is a walk.

**`dated` returns after two empty years.**
[Forensic container checkpointing in Kubernetes](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2022/12/05/forensic-container-checkpointing-alpha/)
is a complete procedure — POST to the kubelet, take the CRIU archive, rebuild it as an image — and
it needs CRIU in the runtime and root on the node, which is past the ceiling these labs are built
to. Both tests hold, the vendor rule does not reach it first, and it books cleanly. `dated` now
stands at 12 rows in 541.

Four things in the topic tallies are worth the taxonomy's attention. **`nodes` takes 12 rows and
3 `walk`s, records for both** — a year of kubelet work, from cgroup v2 to user namespaces.
**`storage` takes 10 rows, also a record, and only 1 `walk`**: the storage posts of 2022 are CSI
driver-side work, and a census whose lab has one node and one provisioner cannot reach most of it.
**`sched` takes 3 rows and 2 `walk`s** after being empty in 2021, matching 2016 for the most
scheduling exercises in a year. And **`etcd` is empty again**: across 541 censused rows the topic
has three rows and has never once produced an exercise, which is the longest-running hole in the
vocabulary. `ecosystem` keeps its own record intact — 64 rows across nine years, zero `walk`s,
exactly as the genre rule requires.
